## 0. Predictions (written BEFORE any engine re-run for the hardware consequence)

**How blind these are, stated up front.** The workbench already holds a scratch experiment on this
exact question (`workbench/packet-study/hardware-consistency/gbw-scratch-prediction.md` and
`gbw-scratch-results.txt`, read-only): the TS console at native 192 kHz with GBP 1e6 vs 3e6 on the
SD-1 and TS-9, ngspice-free, no repo change. I read it before writing the lines below, so these
predictions are *informed by that run*, not independent of it. What this task adds over it is the
general mechanism (typed field, both consoles, catalog source, diagnostic), the controls, and a
re-measurement through the real implementation rather than a patched constant. Nothing from the
scratch is reused as a number: every figure in this note is measured here.

Prediction, for the engine columns of the three specimen tables once each op-amp section carries its
datasheet typical GBP (NJM4558D and uPC4558C, both expected ~3 MHz subject to the datasheet read in
section 3):

- **P1 (direction).** The SD-1 and TS-9 engine level at 12.5 / 16 kHz, relative to 1 kHz, rises, so the
  hardware-minus-engine shape residual shrinks at 8-16 kHz. At 4 kHz the move is small (order 0.1-0.2 dB).
- **P2 (gain dependence).** The move is larger at Drive max / centre than at Drive min. For the SD-1 at
  Drive min it is small (order 0.2-0.3 dB at 16 kHz), because the stage's closed-loop corner
  `GBP / (1 + Rf/Rg)` is far above the audio band when the gain is low.
- **P3 (size, from the closed-loop corner).** A gain-100 stage's corner moves 10 kHz -> 30 kHz with
  GBP 1 -> 3 MHz, so at 16 kHz the stage's droop falls from about -2 dB to about -0.5 dB: an engine
  rise of order 1-1.7 dB at 16 kHz in the high-gain states, 0.4-0.5 dB at 8 kHz, ~1 dB at 12.5 kHz.
- **P4 (over-correction is possible).** The TS-9's residual at 16 kHz is only +1.7 dB in the
  centre/drive-max states and +0.25 dB at Drive min, so a ~1.4-1.7 dB engine rise can carry the TS-9's
  Drive-min and Tone states *through zero* (hardware slightly BELOW the engine, order -0.1 to -0.3 dB).
  That would be reported as a result, not tuned away.
- **P5 (the SD-1 keeps most of its residual).** The SD-1's 16 kHz residual is +3.1 dB, so after a
  ~1.3 dB engine rise roughly +1.7-1.9 dB remains: GBP is part of the gap, not all of it.
- **P6 (Muff).** The Big Muff has no op-amp section: every output hash is bit-identical to baseline.

Pooled-instrument prediction (`pool.py`, hardware minus 192 kHz engine, level removed, median over
states, pedal = unit): at 12.5 / 16 kHz the verdict row moves from **LEAN-COMMON +1.74 / +2.45**
toward a smaller common lift or to PEDAL-SPECIFIC (the TS-9 residual falls below the 0.5 dB floor while
the SD-1 stays above it, which is the verdict code's PEDAL-SPECIFIC branch). I expect the SD-1
residual to stay above the floor at 16 kHz and the TS-9's to drop below it.

**Falsifiers** (any one means GBP is not the explanation, or not the whole of it, for the HF
residual; the change itself is still justified by the datasheet, not by this):

- F1: the engine change at 16 kHz is < 0.2 dB on both pedals (the constant does not matter here).
- F2: the engine overshoots, hardware ends up BELOW the engine by > 0.5 dB at 16 kHz in the
  high-gain states.
- F3: the change is as large at Drive min as at Drive max (not gain-dependent, so not GBP).
- F4: the Muff moves at all (the harness is not isolating the op-amp).

(F1-F4 are the scratch's own falsifiers, adopted unchanged so the two runs can be compared on the
same terms.)

## 2. Control 2(a), the known answer: a STOP condition, and what was decided

**Setup.** A non-inverting stage with exact resistors (`packages/runtime/tests/non-inverting-stage.ts`), gain
G = 1 + Rf/Rg = 10 and 100, product stated as a typed `GBP` of 1e6, 3e6 and 1e7 Hz in the packet text, driven at os4
(48 kHz host), 10 mV, TS and WASM consoles. Analytic closed loop with the A0 correction: `Acl = G0 / (1 + j f/fc)`,
`G0 = G / (1 + G/A0)`, `fc = GBP (1/A0 + 1/G)` (A0 = the 1e5 class default).

**Result: the brief's bar (within 0.05 dB of the continuous closed form below 10 kHz) FAILS on both consoles, identically,
for a reason that is not the wiring.** Measured minus the continuous closed form, os4 (`logs/02a-*`):

```
G    GBP    | 2 kHz  4 kHz  8 kHz  10 kHz  16 kHz   (dB, TS = WASM to the printed digit)
10   1 MHz  | +0.000 -0.023 -0.086 -0.134  -0.326
10   3 MHz  | +0.004 -0.008 -0.028 -0.044  -0.111
100  1 MHz  | -0.050 -0.194 -0.514 -0.639  -0.840
100  3 MHz  | -0.013 -0.074 -0.271 -0.400  -0.817
100  10 MHz | +0.000 -0.023 -0.087 -0.135  -0.327
```

**Cause.** The op-amp pole is backward-Euler (`opAmpPoleAlpha`: `alpha = dt/(dt+tau)`, raw state
`v[n] = alpha*A0*d[n] + (1-alpha)*v[n-1]`). In a closed loop of feedback fraction `beta = 1/G` that is exactly
`H(z) = K / (1 - a z^-1)`, `K = alpha A0 / (1 + alpha A0 beta)`, `a = (1-alpha) / (1 + alpha A0 beta)`, whose
magnitude departs from the continuous pole by about `w^2 tau dt`. Three checks that this is the cause and not the
field: (i) **unchanged main** (no field, the 1e6 constant) shows the identical -0.639 dB at 10 kHz for G=100
(`logs/02a-analytic-stage-baseline-main.log`); (ii) both consoles match the **BE-discrete closed form** to within
0.006 dB over 6 stages x 9 frequencies x 2 consoles (`logs/02a-measured-vs-continuous-vs-BE-os4.log`); (iii) the
error scales with dt.

**Decision (coordinator, on the evidence above; recorded verbatim in substance).** `continue-2`, with: control 2(a) is
SPLIT, never relabelled. *(a-wire)* measured vs the BE-discrete closed form <= 0.01 dB on both consoles across all
stages and frequencies, plus the declared-value move gate (3 MHz minus 1 MHz measured equals the BE-form difference
within 0.02 dB): this proves the field reaches the solver and proves nothing about accuracy. *(a-accuracy)* measured vs
the continuous analytic stays in this note as a **FAILING control**, titled a standing finding about the BE pole
discretisation: not tuned, not changed, not reported as passed, and its failure leads the worker_done summary. No other
bar is loosened and `opAmpPoleAlpha` and the pole state update are untouched in the diff. The hardware section
separates the two mechanisms (GBP vs BE error) with a scratch-only rate scan.

### 3.2 Why it was reverted before, and whether that still applies

The `netlist.ts` comment said the product had "no consumer until a dominant pole exists, and that was implemented and
reverted". The history is in the workbench (core's own history starts after it, at the squash `cc19fb9`):

- `3152ba88` (2026-08-13) implemented the pole in both the runtime and the ngspice deck and **reverted it against its own
  gate**: the parity count fell from 32 agrees to 26 (six rows that agreed no longer did). `f7861d7e` had reverted a
  deck-only version the same week.
- `17b004d1` (2026-08-13) localised part of the mismatch: a pole puts a closed-loop pole at `GBW / A_closed`, so a
  unity-gain buffer at 1 MHz carries a 1 MHz pole, forty times Nyquist at 48 kHz, which a 48 kHz solver cannot
  represent; "oversampling is a prerequisite, not an optimisation".
- `f355c289` (2026-08-21) **landed the self-consistent pole** (backward-Euler raw state in the runtime, an RC pole node in
  the deck): 39 agrees at 1x, both negative controls back to correlation 1.0000.

So the stated reason (no consumer) is **stale**: the pole has shipped for seven weeks and both consoles read a product
from it. One part of the 08-13 diagnosis **still applies and is measured here**: a closed-loop pole above Nyquist is not
representable at x1, and the backward-Euler pole is low against the continuous one by about `w^2 tau dt` (section 2). That
is a property of the pole model, present at the 1 MHz default, not a reason to leave the product unread.

a spec-derived voltage scale, one level (10 mV). A shrinking residual does not prove the product is the physical cause of the rest:
the engine's single pole, its BE discretisation, generic silicon BJT parameters and the packets' device values are all still in
the way, and the SD-1's HF gap (+3.15 dB at 16 kHz) is untouched by this change. The 3 MHz the TS-9 gets is the Nisshinbo
datasheet's TYP column for the NJM4558, a figure for the part and not a measurement of this specimen; a specimen with a -D rank or a

## 10. Public surface (every addition, for the owner; versions will be compiler 0.5.0 and runtime 0.5.0, not bumped here)

**`@vessel-dsp/compiler`** (`export * from "./types"` is the public type surface):
1. `Stamp` union: the `ideal-opamp` member gains **`gainBandwidthHz: number | null`, required**. A caller that builds an op-amp stamp by hand
   no longer typechecks (two test fixtures needed `gainBandwidthHz: null`).
2. `DeviceLaw` union: the `ideal-opamp` member gains optional `gainBandwidthHz?: number`.
3. `CompileWarning` union: new member `OpampGainBandwidthDefaultWarning` (`code: "opamp-gain-bandwidth-default"`, `device: null`,
   `devices`, `sections`, `detail`). It is emitted for 49 of the 145 corpus documents, so any consumer that switches exhaustively on warning
   codes sees a new case.
4. The serialized program (`emit(program).text`) gains a `"gainBandwidthHz": null | <hertz>` member on every `ideal-opamp` stamp, so **the digest of every
   program holding an op-amp changes (87 of 145 corpus documents)** even where the value is `null`; the programs are otherwise byte-identical to main.
5. Behavioural: an `opamp` component's typed `GBP` is now read, and a declared `GBP` that is not a frequency (a number with Hz/kHz/MHz/GHz, a bare number,
   or `{value, unit}`) **refuses the compile by name** (`NetlistError`) rather than being ignored. No corpus document does this.
6. Not public (module-level, reachable only by deep import): `registryOpampGainBandwidth`, `typedFrequencyHz`, `opampGainBandwidthDatasheet`,
   `opampGainBandwidthAbsent`, `OpampGainBandwidthRow`.

**`@vessel-dsp/runtime`**: no exported symbol changes. `ReferenceRuntime` and `V2WasmEngine` now read the stamp field.
**C++ (`Program.h`, `ProgramJson.cpp`, `Engine.cpp`)**: `Stamp::gainBandwidthHz` (`std::optional<double>`; JSON `null` or a missing key is absent), the
constant renamed `OPAMP_DEFAULT_GAIN_BANDWIDTH_HZ`, one helper `opAmpGainBandwidthHz(stamp)`. `GeneratedKernels.cpp` is unchanged (section 11).
**`@vessel-dsp/chain`, `@vessel-dsp/player`**: no change (they re-pin).

**Does the Program have a schema or version a stale runtime can check, and what happens to a 0.4.x runtime?** It has `formatVersion` (6) and
`requiredOperators` / `requiredModels`, the declared instruction-set surface a runtime refuses by name at load. **Neither sees this field**: the key is a new member of an
existing stamp kind (`ideal-opamp` is already declared), and 0.4.x consoles ignore unknown stamp keys. Measured (`stale-runtime.ts`, the baseline
tree, a program exported by this compiler for the TS-9 with both stamps `gainBandwidthHz: 3000000`, `logs/08-stale-runtime.log`): it loads without a refusal or a warning and renders
with **exactly the unchanged-main hashes** on both consoles at x1 and os4, i.e. **it silently plays the 1 MHz default**. The opposite direction is handled and
tested: a new console given a pre-field program (no key) reads it as `null`, which is what that program was compiled against. The README's rule
("any change to `Program`, the `Stamp` union, `ProgramJson.cpp` or `Program.h` is a package minor bump") is why 0.5.0 is proposed. **Owner decision: this is exactly the
case the earlier format bumps were made for ("a version 4 reader would play every knob ... silently").** Options, none implemented: (a) leave `formatVersion` 6 and accept
the silent default on stale runtimes; (b) bump to 7, so a stale runtime refuses *every* program, op-amp or not; (c) add a capability to the version surface that a program
declares only when some stamp states a product (exact in the way `requiredOperators` is), so a stale runtime refuses exactly the programs it would misplay. (c) is the
finest-grained and is my recommendation if the silent default is not acceptable.

## 13. What this cannot prove

- **The hardware consequence** is n = 3 pedals (one without an op-amp, one without a datasheet figure), one rig, one level (10 mV), knobs by eye, a spec-derived voltage scale; a smaller residual does not
  make the datasheet's TYP 3 MHz the physical cause of the rest, and the SD-1's +3.15 dB at 16 kHz is untouched.
- **Accuracy of the pole** is not established: only (a-wire) passes, (a-accuracy) fails (section 6.1), ngspice carries the same single-pole model, and agreement with it is "necessary, never proof of a real-device match".
- **Datasheet TYP figures** are figures for the part family, at the conditions printed (some at +-15 V, one at 5 V, the NE5534 with a 22 pF compensation capacitor); a specimen, a suffix or a vintage can differ. The TL07x/TL08x 5.25 MHz is the current TI sheet's figure for DIP and SOIC-D parts and is
  higher than the 3 MHz older sheets, the curated records and the registry carry; the corpus packets that declare 3 MHz win by precedence.
- **The corpus movement** is one stimulus per cell (a 100 mV sine at three frequencies at two factors); it is not a corpus-wide listening or hardware comparison, and several large moves (`boss-aw-2`, `dod-envelope-filter`) are unverified engine consequences.
- **Stall behaviour** is deterministic per input and per binary but trajectory-sensitive; the canary is one input. No claim is made about the mechanism of the iteration-cap storms.
- **Timing** is one idle-ish window (load 1.29-2.56) on a shared box; it is information, not a gate.
